# CYBER

## Overview

The B.E. Computer Science and Engineering with Specialization in Cyber Security, or CSCS, is an undergraduate engineering degree program that aims to equip students with the knowledge and skills necessary to protect computer systems, networks, and data from cyber threats and attacks. As the world becomes increasingly reliant on digital infrastructure across various sectors, the field of cyber security has become critical, and this course is designed to meet the growing demand for professionals in this area.

The CSCS program boasts a specialized curriculum that focuses on key security principles, including network security, cryptography, ethical hacking, and digital forensics. In addition to these specialized courses, the program also includes foundational courses in computer science, such as Data Structures, Algorithms, and Operating Systems. To provide students with hands-on experience, the program features labs and simulation environments where students can engage in penetration testing, secure coding, and threat analysis. Furthermore, the program includes capstone projects, internships, and real-world problem-solving opportunities to help students apply their knowledge in practical settings.

The curriculum also places a strong emphasis on legal and ethical foundations, integrating cyber laws and ethics, compliance, and governance into the program. Moreover, the program explores emerging technologies, including security in AI, IoT, Cloud computing, and Blockchain. Top recruiters recognize the value of this program, and graduates can expect to be in high demand.

The B.E. in CSE - Cyber Security program is offered in two formats: a four-year regular program and a three-year lateral entry program. The regular program consists of eight semesters, while the lateral entry program consists of six semesters. The program has a total intake of 30 students, with 15 seats allocated to government quota and 15 seats allocated to management quota.

The Mohamed Sathak A.J. College of Engineering adheres to the 10+2 system of education, which serves as the foundation for its academic programs. To be eligible for admission, prospective students must have secured a pass in the core subjects of Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics in their qualifying examination, demonstrating a strong grasp of these fundamental disciplines.

The college's curriculum and syllabus are designed in accordance with the 2021 regulation, ensuring that students receive a comprehensive education that is aligned with the latest industry trends and academic standards. This rigorous academic framework provides students with a solid foundation for their future careers and also opens up scope for higher studies, enabling them to pursue advanced degrees and specializations in their chosen fields.

In the realm of cyber security, various roles exist to safeguard organizations from threats and breaches. A Cyber Security Analyst is the first line of defense, responsible for monitoring network traffic, identifying security breaches, and implementing protective measures. To excel in this role, one must possess skills in network security, incident response, SIEM tools, firewall management, and penetration testing.

A Security Architect, on the other hand, designs and implements complex security infrastructures to protect IT systems, networks, and data. This role often involves setting up secure frameworks for cloud, IoT, and enterprise networks, requiring expertise in network architecture, security frameworks, risk management, cloud security, firewalls, and encryption technologies.

Penetration Testers, also known as Ethical Hackers, conduct penetration tests to identify and exploit vulnerabilities in an organization's systems. They simulate cyber attacks to help organizations find weaknesses before malicious hackers can exploit them. To succeed in this role, one must be proficient in Kali Linux, Meta sploit, Wire shark, nmap, vulnerability scanning tools, and hold an ethical hacking certification such as CEH or OSCP.

A Security Operations Center (SOC) Analyst monitors an organization's network for potential security threats and responds to security incidents in real-time. SOC analysts work to ensure minimal downtime and risk, requiring skills in threat analysis, log analysis, incident detection and response, and SIEM systems such as Splunk and IBM QRadar.

Cloud Security Specialists ensure the security of cloud infrastructures and applications, focusing on preventing unauthorized access, data breaches, and ensuring compliance with cloud-specific security protocols. They must be knowledgeable in AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, cloud security best practices, identity and access management, and encryption.

Cyber Security Consultants provide expertise to organizations on how to improve their overall cyber security posture. They assess vulnerabilities, provide strategic recommendations, and help implement security solutions, requiring skills in risk assessment, security audits, compliance frameworks, penetration testing, and security policy development.

In the event of a cyber security incident, Incident Responders investigate and respond to data breaches and malware attacks, working quickly to mitigate damage and prevent further issues. They must possess skills in incident handling, forensics, malware analysis, root cause analysis, and digital forensics tools.

Cyber Security Engineers build and maintain security systems, firewalls, and encryption technologies to protect organizational assets. This role involves proactively designing systems to prevent breaches, requiring expertise in firewall configuration, endpoint protection, network security protocols, and security software development.

At the helm of an organization's cyber security strategy is the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The CISO oversees policies, systems, and incident response, ensuring the company is compliant with relevant regulations and industry standards. To excel in this role, one must possess leadership skills, risk management expertise, compliance knowledge, security program management skills, and business continuity planning expertise.

Lastly, IoT Security Experts protect connected devices in IoT ecosystems from cyber threats, ensuring that IoT devices are secure, encrypted, and do not pose risks to organizational infrastructure. They must be knowledgeable in IoT protocols, embedded security, network security, data encryption, and IoT device security.

Cyber security professionals can expect to find numerous job opportunities in various sectors, particularly in IT and IT Services companies that offer IT solutions and managed services.
