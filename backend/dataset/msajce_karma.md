# KARMA

## Kaushal Augmentation and Restructuring Mission of AICTE (KARMA)

The All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) has launched the "Kaushal Augmentation and Restructuring Mission of AICTE" (KARMA), a nationwide initiative aimed at addressing the pressing issues of skilled manpower scarcity and low skill levels in the workforce. The primary objectives of KARMA are multifaceted, with a key focus on creating a skilled and certified workforce through strategically planned skill initiatives. This endeavor not only seeks to contribute to India's growth but also aspires to establish the country as the global skills capital.

To achieve this vision, KARMA encourages institutions to optimize their existing infrastructure during off-hours for skill training. This involves realigning infrastructure, training aids, syllabi, and curricula to align with National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF) standards. The scheme is open to candidates who have passed at least the 10th standard or completed ITI courses. The training programs under KARMA will focus on domain-specific, demand-led skill training activities, preferably in core engineering sectors, with a primary goal of leading to employment opportunities.

AICTE has proposed three distinct skilling models under the KARMA initiative. The first model targets school dropouts who have passed their 10th standard, including both recent and long-time dropouts.

The Kaushal Augmentation and Restructuring Mission of AICTE, also known as KARMA, has introduced two models aimed at enhancing the skills of students in technical institutions. Model 2 focuses on targeting advance level skilling of students already studying in AICTE-approved technical institutions. This program is designed to upgrade the skills of present technical students, making them industry-ready by offering advanced skilling courses during their 4th to 7th semester. The assessment and other criteria for this program will be similar to those of KARMA.

In another initiative, Model 3 seeks to integrate school education with higher education for skilling students. Recognizing the need for a shift from corrective to connective and transactional to relational education, AICTE is encouraging its institutions to establish a connection between higher education institutions and schools. To achieve this, AICTE suggests that its institutions start offering skill-based training to nearby school students once a week, following a "Hub and Spoke" model.

Mohamed Sathak A J College of Engineering has been approved by AICTE to conduct five courses under Model 2, with a specified intake for each course. These courses include AI-Machine Learning Developer, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Technician, Additive Manufacturing Technician, which focuses on 3D printing, Certificate Course in Embedded System Design using ARM/Cortex Microcontroller, and Architectural Drafting and Basic 3D Design with Autodesk Revit.
